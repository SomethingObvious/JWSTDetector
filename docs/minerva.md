# The MINERVA Search

This is the plan for searching MINERVA (JWST program 7814, Muzzin et al. 2025) the way [search.md](search.md) searched COSMOS-Web, written before any of its deep tiles are scored, with what COSMOS-Web taught built in. Where it doesn't say otherwise, the rules are COSMOS-Web's.

The plan as it stood before scoring is this page at commit 8ba435a, pushed on 2026-11-08. Every change to it since is listed in [Changes to the Plan After Scoring](#changes-to-the-plan-after-scoring), and the Result and the Rescore were written after the verdict.

## Data

The four deep mosaics, o001 and o002 in UDS and o003 and o004 in COSMOS, as STScI's level-3 i2d files from MAST, with F460M, F250M and F182M as red, green and blue. They're 22,000 to 30,000 s deep in each filter. The AEGIS visits are shallower and are the model confirmation set ([selection.md](selection.md#the-model-confirmation-on-aegis)), so they're not in this search.

Prep is COSMOS-Web's (320 px tiles every 160 px on the F460M grid, 0.063 arcsec a pixel), except that each filter's stretch is the median over the four mosaics (`--levels`, written from a first cut of the same four with their own stretch).

## Blocks Instead of Visits

Four mosaics are far too few to calibrate visit against visit, so each one is split into square blocks 1,920 px (about 2 arcminutes) a side, and the blocks play the part COSMOS-Web's visits played (`cut_blocks` in [survey.py](../jwst_peculiar/survey.py)). A block takes a tile only if all of it lies inside, so no two blocks share a pixel. The tiles that straddle a border belong to none, though a block's own edge tiles still reach its border. A full block holds 121 tiles, and one with fewer than 30 is left out. The blocks are sorted by name (mosaic, row, column) and shuffled with seed 0. Then:

- the first 15 blocks are the kNN bank,
- the next blocks, as many as keep the search's tiles at 1/12 of the search's and calibration's together or fewer, are the search,
- and the rest calibrate.

The overlap rule drops any search or calibration tile that shares sky with a tile of another block, the bank's included. Two tiles of one mosaic overlap only if their boxes do, which blocks never do, so this only bites where two mosaics meet. It also drops any tile on sky the adopted model was trained on (`training_data` in the split), as the fine-tune has seen that sky as normal.

## Scoring and the Verdict

Every block is scored in bank mode against the bank with the model the AEGIS confirmation keeps. Each block's kept scores go on its own median and MAD (`"standardise": true`) before the conformal p, and the rest is COSMOS-Web's: BH at q <= 0.1 over every kept search tile, and a rehearsal on calibration blocks alone that has to find something in at most 15% of draws.

The star rule reaches fainter stars than COSMOS-Web's did. [minerva-stars.json](minerva-stars.json) holds every Gaia DR3 source brighter than G = 16 over the four mosaics, and the ones from G = 16 to 19 that Gaia sees moving (proper motion or parallax over 5 sigma), since compact galaxies and quasars don't move and nearly every Gaia source that bright is a star. The split sets `"star_g_max": 19`, and the reach is COSMOS-Web's. The cut-off came from COSMOS-Web's scores after its verdict. With the overlap rule alone, the old rule dropped 13.5% of its tiles and 44.5% of the top 1%, and this one drops 20.7% and 57.7% (and 61.7% of the top 0.1%, against 38.3%). Going fainter than G = 19 dropped tiles barely faster than chance. Of the 20 diffraction spikes among the trial list's 50 sources (below), it drops 14.

## The Vetting List

The test above can only name a tile that beats nearly every calibration tile, so most of what the search sees never reaches it. A trial on COSMOS-Web's 50 highest sources, ranked as below after its verdict, found 20 diffraction spikes, 9 detector artefacts (mostly in F150W alone), 4 trails, 6 edges of bright galaxies cut off by the tile, 9 ordinary galaxies and pairs, and 2 red compact galaxies that are already catalogued. Nothing in it was new, and two thirds of it was something a person would throw out in seconds, which is what the star rule above and the flag below are for.

[vet.py](../jwst_peculiar/vet.py) writes the list for MINERVA, once the verdict is in. It ranks every scored block, calibration as well as search, since the list makes no statistical claim, after the overlap and star rules and on each block's median and MAD. Each source is listed once, at its highest tile (hot spots within 1.5 arcsec are one source). Its hottest patch is checked in each filter of the tile's cutout, as MADs off the tile for both the patch mean and its smoothed peak, and it's flagged if it shows in no filter (all under 5) or in one filter only (that one at least 1.6 times the next and the next under 8). The flagged ones are listed after the 50 that aren't. On COSMOS-Web's trial list, where these numbers were picked, the flag caught 8 of the 13 artefacts and trails and 1 of the 17 real sources. Every source gets a sheet with the tile, each filter and its hot spot in colour, and the nearest SIMBAD and NED entries within 2 arcsec.

## The Colour Search

The model sees three filters as a picture, but MINERVA has eight medium bands, and what most often stands out in medium bands is a spectrum, such as an emission line bright enough to light up one band, which a picture of three bands can miss. So a second list ranks sources by how unlike every well-measured source their spectrum is. It's unsupervised like the first and makes no claim either.

- All eight bands of the four deep mosaics (F140M, F162M, F182M, F210M, F250M, F300M, F360M and F460M), each smoothed with a Gaussian to F460M's resolution, using the PSF widths in JDox, and resampled onto the F460M grid.
- Each band loses a local sky, a clipped median in boxes about 6 arcsec across, since the halo of a bright star reaches well past the star rule and turned whole regions into one source. Sources are found with photutils on the noise-weighted sum of the eight bands (5 connected pixels over 3 sigma), deblended, and placed at their segment's peak rather than its centroid, as a segment can hold a source and a stretch of halo or detector stripe beside it. Each is measured in a 0.32 arcsec aperture in each band. A band's error is the scatter of the same aperture on empty sky in its 1,920 px block, with 5% of the flux added in quadrature for PSF and calibration mismatch. Sources need every pixel within 0.24 arcsec covered in all eight bands, 10 sigma in the sum, and to be outside a bright star's halo (the star rule's reach without the tile's half-diagonal, as a source is a point, which takes 2.3% of o003 instead of 15.6%).
- The sources at 30 sigma or more in every band form the library. Each source's score is the fifth-smallest chi-squared over library sources (leaving out itself and anything within 1 arcsec), with the library spectrum scaled to fit, so a kind of spectrum needs five well-measured examples before it counts as ordinary, and a faint source with big errors can't score high on noise alone.
- A source more concentrated than a star is left out of the ranking and counted. The flux inside 0.08 arcsec over the flux inside 0.24 arcsec puts o003's point sources at 0.35 to 0.40, and above 0.45 sat 412 of its 526 sources scoring over 300, against 2 of 16,223 scoring under 30. Those are hot pixels and cosmic rays in one band, which score about 400 because one band off by its whole flux costs (1 / 0.05) squared.
- This rule and the next were added on 2026-11-09, after the first full list had been looked at. Smoothing spreads a hot pixel into something star shaped, which gets it past that, so each band's own pixels are checked first. A pixel over 8 sigma that's the brightest in its 3 by 3 box and holds more than 0.32 of the box's flux is taken as a hot pixel, as 60 library stars put at most 0.28 there, and a band with one inside the aperture counts as unmeasured. On the first full list's top 20, this took 7 of its 13 artefacts and 2 of its 5 unclear sources out of the top 250, and its two real sources (the brown dwarf candidate below is one) stayed 1st and 3rd. A cosmic ray across 2 by 2 pixels or more is spread too thin for it, though, and 10 of the 14 sources that moved up into the top 24 were one of those.
- So a band other than F460M that holds 20 times every other band (or its 1 sigma, where a band has nothing) also counts as unmeasured. [OIII] at a rest equivalent width of 4,000 A, about the most extreme known, lifts a medium band to at most 12 times the rest, and in Sonora Bobcat only F460M goes further, to 2,500 times F360M at 200 K, which is why F460M is left alone. It takes a band from 850 of the 73,491 sources and from none of the 7,787 in the library. Of the 16 artefacts in that top 24, 11 fell past 2,000th and the four real sources kept their places. The two lone F460M hits stay on purpose, and so do three single-band excesses of 4 to 20 times, which an emission line can match. Fainter cosmic rays stay as well, where the other bands are too noisy to show the source isn't in them.
- The 50 highest sources (once each) get a sheet with all eight bands and their spectrum against the five library fits, plus the catalogue match, and go after the first list.

There's one known answer in o003. Festin et al. (2026) list a brown dwarf candidate from COSMOS-Web at 150.06343 +2.35522, and it's the highest source on o003's list, 0.23 arcsec from where COSMOS-Web saw it, as it has moved since. With only medium bands, though, a hotter T dwarf and a galaxy at z of about 2.3 with [OIII] in F162M and H-alpha in F210M look alike, so this list doesn't call anything a brown dwarf by itself.

## What It Claims

Only search tiles with q <= 0.1 count as detections. Anything else, both lists included, is a candidate for a person to look at and nothing more. A detection gets looked at in every filter and matched against catalogues, and nothing is said about it to anyone outside before that, or before the MINERVA team has seen it. The lists are looked at the same way, and only what survives a first look goes any further.

## Changes to the Plan After Scoring

These are every edit to the sections above since 8ba435a, apart from the package's rename and two references added.

- The colour search's hot pixel rule and its rule for one band 20 times the rest were added on 2026-11-09, once the first full list had been looked at. They only change the colour list, which makes no claim.
- The Blocks section said the tiles straddling a border leave a 160 px strip along it unsearched. With `owned` scoring, which the search used, a block's edge tiles score out to its border, so none of it went unsearched. Only the later rescore on tile middles leaves 80 px inside each border unscored.
- Scoring and the Verdict said BH ran over the search's grid tiles. The 320 px grid rule had been dropped before this plan (see [search.md](search.md)), and the verdict counted every kept search tile.

## The Result

Every block was scored on 2026-11-09, and `results_minerva/verdict.json` has the numbers. After the overlap and star rules, 514 tiles in 8 search blocks were tested against 3,838 calibration tiles (2,283 tiles were dropped as overlaps and 724 as near a bright star). The rehearsal passed, with 5.9% of its draws getting any detection, and nothing in the search reached $q \le 0.1$. The lowest p of a search tile was 0.006. Each rehearsal draw takes 8 calibration blocks as the search, as the plan says, but with the blocks the rules emptied a draw tests about 365 tiles on average against the search's 514, so it doesn't copy the search's numbers exactly.

It could never have named one tile alone, though. The smallest p any tile can get is 1 over the calibration tiles plus one, 0.00026, and BH needed 0.00019 to name a single tile. Two tiles that each beat every calibration tile would both have passed, as BH needs 0.00039 for a pair, but nothing short of that could have shown. The split kept the search at 561 of 7,360 tiles (7.6%), but that was counted before the overlap and star rules, which left it at 514 of 4,352 (11.8%), past the 1/11 at which one tile alone can pass. The overlap rule emptied 16 calibration blocks and the star rule one more, so only 66 of the 84 calibration blocks took part, and none of that was checked before scoring. So an empty result says little here, and the lists above are what the search is good for. A block left with fewer than two tiles has no spread to standardise on, so it's left out (one block kept a single tile). That rule depends on the tile counts alone, but it went into the code while the blocks were being scored and was committed with the result, so git can't show it came before any score was seen. Scoring also turned up a bug that refused blocks of one mosaic as two mosaics sharing a name, which was fixed before the verdict.

So the survey now checks this first. `python -m jwst_peculiar.survey --check SPLIT STARS DATA` runs the overlap and star rules on the tile list alone, with no scores, exits with 1 if the best possible p couldn't name a tile alone, and says how many tiles would have to reach it together. On this split it gives the same 514 and 3,838 the verdict did. A second search of these four mosaics wouldn't count, though, since every block's scores and the lists above have been looked at. The next one needs sky nobody has scored yet, and a split that passes the check before anything is scored.

Where two mosaics share sky, the overlap rule drops both copies of a tile, and one copy would have been enough to keep an object from being its own twin. A split with `"keep_first_copy": true` drops only the tile of the block whose name sorts later, which depends on the names alone and so treats search and calibration blocks alike. On this split it would have left 4,555 calibration tiles to 519 search tiles, which still couldn't name one tile alone.

The four lowest p's are neighbouring tiles in o003 around 150.083 +2.203. On the vetting list that spot is a faint galaxy at the edge of a tile split by a seam where the background changes, so it's most likely the seam that scored. The top of the vetting list is SL2S J021737-051329, a strong lens already known from CFHT, with its arc clear in all three filters, and most of the rest near the top are the insides of bright nearby galaxies.

## A Rescore After the Result

This section was added on 2026-11-09, after the verdict was in and the lists had been looked at, so nothing in it is a pre-registered result. The verdict above stands as it was written.

Cutting the mosaics into blocks changed what `--score_region owned` does. A tile scores only its middle 14 by 14 patches when all eight of its neighbours are there to take the rest, but a tile on a block's border or beside a hole in the coverage has no neighbour on that side, so it scores out to its own edge. That was the case for 2,838 of the 4,352 kept tiles (65%). Those tiles score more patches, and the extra ones are the nearest to the edge of the image the backbone sees, which is what [owned scoring](method.md#which-patches-a-tile-scores) was meant to keep out. It showed in the ranking. Their median standardised score was +0.14 against -0.29 for tiles with every neighbour, and they were 38 of the 44 highest tiles, where their share of the tiles would give 29.

So every tile was scored again on its middle 196 patches alone, from the anomaly maps the run had saved and with no model run. `python -m jwst_peculiar.survey --middle SPLIT STARS DATA RESULTS` does that, and it writes `verdict_middle.json` and `search_pvalues_middle.csv` beside the verdict without touching it. The same 514 and 3,838 tiles are tested, the rehearsal passes with 9.4% of its draws getting any detection, and nothing in the search reaches $q \le 0.1$. The lowest p of a search tile is 0.006 again, though on other tiles (two neighbours in o001 and one in o004). The four o003 tiles that had the lowest p's now rank 18th to 46th with p from 0.03 to 0.08, which fits the guess above that it was a seam at a tile's edge that scored.

There is a control with a known answer for whether the rescore fixed this. Among tiles whose eight neighbours all exist in the uncut mosaic, the sky is the same kind whether or not the block cut took a neighbour away, so any gap between those two groups is the scoring's doing. As first scored, the cut tiles' median sat 0.41 above the others' in the same block on average, and it was above in 40 of the 47 blocks that have at least 5 tiles of each kind. On the middle the average gap is 0.03 and it's above in 23 of the 47 (a two-sided sign-flip test over blocks gives p = 0.75, and 0.37 one-sided in the direction of the first gap).

Tiles with a neighbour missing are still a bit over-represented at the top afterwards, at 34 of the 44 highest. That is mostly the tiles beside a hole in the coverage, 6.2% of which are in the top 5% against 3.6% of the tiles with every neighbour. It isn't the scoring. On the middle, a tile scores the same 196 patches against the same bank whether its neighbours exist or not, so a missing neighbour can't move its score. Most of these holes aren't gaps in the sky either. Prep drops a window if any pixel in it has no weight in any filter, and the small holes of one to three windows were dropped over a few F182M pixels the pipeline left without weight around satellite trails, saturated stars and wisps, which then light up the tile next door. Tiles beside holes that small are in the top 5% about 11% of the time, against about 5.5% beside the large holes at the edges of the coverage. Of the 48 highest tiles beside a hole, about 14 show a satellite trail, 11 the halo of a bright galaxy or star and 5 a wisp in F182M. So the excess is artefacts that are really there, and nothing was changed for it. One smaller thing turned up on the way. 40 kept tiles have pixels with no weight inside their middle, which prep counts as covered when they're enclosed, and 25 of them have their hottest patch there. Leaving those patches out moves the tiles beside holes from 6.2% to 6.1% of the top 5%.

Scoring the middle isn't free, as a tile with no neighbour leaves its outer patches scored by nothing, and the patches scored fell from 1,327,720 to 852,992. So `--score_region middle` is the one for comparing tiles that don't all have neighbours, and `owned` is still the one that covers the sky.
